# Formative learning evaluation — pilot protocol

Status: proposed protocol, no participant results. Intended audience: pre-clinical medical students, particularly years 1–2. Start with a small formative round (for example 4–6 volunteers) to find usability problems, then revise before designing a powered comparative study. Obtain applicable institutional approval/consent before recruitment or recording. No personal or health information is needed in the app.

## Preparation

An anatomy/embryology educator signs off the cushion-fusion objective, diagram correspondence, answer rationales and coarctation explanation. A congenital cardiology expert reviews the difference between observable narrowing, proposed mechanisms and clinical consequences. Select a licensed unfamiliar image/diagram with a defensible answer before assessing transfer. Use a comparable baseline resource and equivalent questions; counterbalance resource order if learners use both.

## Session (approximately 20–25 minutes)

1. Record prior exposure as a broad category, not examination grades or identifiers. Give a short baseline question on spatial relationships, one developmental explanation and an uncued abnormality-localisation item.
2. Ask the learner to inspect, enlarge, separate and restore anatomy. Observe overhead reaching, lost controls, accidental selection, recovery time and clarity of reset verbs. Repeat after changing lessons and returning home.
3. Cushion pilot: observe the initial state, predict the relationship, reveal the later state, describe the change, then answer recall. Ask which changes the model does and does not demonstrate. Do not count simple advancement as an answer.
4. Coarctation: inspect the normal reference, identify the abnormal region without hints, compare, describe the difference, and explain its possible consequence. Log hints and reveals separately. Ask the learner to separate anatomical observation from causal hypotheses.
5. Transfer only after an expert approves the unfamiliar representation: locate the finding and explain its structural clue. A rotated copy of the original mesh is same-model practice, not evidence of clinical transfer.
6. Collect brief clarity/effort ratings and the learner's explanation of each mode's purpose. Use think-aloud observations to improve layout; do not interpret satisfaction as learning efficacy.

## Immediate and delayed measures

Record viewed, attempted, first unaided correct, assisted correct, retry, skipped, explanation revisited, and unfamiliar-representation response independently. Use an educator-approved 0–2 rubric for spatial relationship, developmental change, anatomical deviation and consequence: 0 incorrect/missing; 1 partly correct; 2 correct with relevant relationships. These rubric categories are a draft; experts must supply anchor answers and acceptable variants.

Offer equivalent recall items after a predefined delay, such as seven days. Treat this as a study-design choice, not an optimal learning interval. Record attrition and prior revisits. Compare change from baseline and with the alternative resource; a small formative sample supports design revisions, not efficacy or clinical-competence claims.

## Stop/revise conditions

Revise misleading explanations, persistent spatial disorientation, answer leakage or repeated inability to recover the model before further learning evaluation. Retain the original 35-issue register and HEADSET_ACCEPTANCE.md for hardware checks.
